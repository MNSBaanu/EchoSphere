# 1. Introduction

## EchoSphere: The World You Shape

**Team Members:** Baanu & Irfa

---

The system is designed within the field of Artificial Intelligence, focusing on agent behaviour and decision-making systems. It presents a 2D interactive environment where an autonomous agent, represented by a teenage student named Kai, navigates between two competing worlds: the digital space (mobile interaction) and the real world (social and personal responsibilities). Built using Phaser.js 3, JavaScript ES6+, and GSAP for animation, EchoSphere demonstrates how AI techniques — including Finite State Machines, emotional intelligence, learning and memory, visual perception, and autonomous agent behaviour — can be applied to model a meaningful real-world problem: the psychological impact of digital addiction on a young person's life.

The simulation begins with a simple moment — Kai in his room, faced with a choice when his phone lights up with a notification. From this point, the system unfolds as a series of interconnected scenarios driven by the agent's decisions. The environment is partially observable, stochastic, and dynamic: NPCs move autonomously, random events alter the world, and the agent's internal state evolves continuously based on accumulated behaviour. Every choice Kai makes — or fails to make — has a measurable consequence tracked through AI variables including addiction level, awareness, relationship level, and emotional state.

If Kai moves toward the phone, he enters the Attraction phase, where engaging digital content begins to influence his behaviour. A social media feed opens, and as Kai scrolls, his addiction level rises, his awareness drops, and the Finite State Machine progresses through increasingly compulsive states — from Attracted, to Looping, to Distorted, and ultimately to a Breaking Point. This can lead to the Addiction phase, where excessive interaction reduces awareness and negatively affects relationships. As a consequence, the agent may reach the Results (Bad Grades) scenario, highlighted when the addiction progress bar reaches 100% and a failure notification is displayed. However, through the system's learning mechanism, the agent remembers these outcomes and is guided toward improvement via a learning key that redirects Kai to the study path.

Alternatively, Kai may choose to focus on his responsibilities, entering the Studies (Learning) phase, where he works toward academic goals while managing distractions. In the Learning Scene, Kai completes two study tasks — Mathematics and Science — by pressing SPACE to fill progress bars, earning XP and reinforcing positive behaviour through particle effects and visual feedback. At any point, he may also choose to step into the Real World, interacting with three NPCs: his mother, his friend Alex, and his sibling Sam. These interactions are governed by a perception system with a directional vision cone (200px, ±45°) and an omnidirectional hearing range (150px), both of which are affected by environmental conditions such as dusk and phone distraction. Engaging with NPCs strengthens Kai's emotional intelligence and helps restore balance by increasing awareness and relationship levels.

The simulation is not linear; instead, it operates as a behavioural loop, where the agent continuously learns from past experiences. The learning system stores behavioural patterns in a persistent memory array — patterns such as high addiction, social neglect, compulsive scrolling, and advice received from his mother — which carry across scene transitions and modify future behaviour. Depending on accumulated decisions, the system leads to different outcomes, including recovery (RECOVERED state), partial improvement (PARTIAL state), or complete loss of connection (LOST state). This design ensures that EchoSphere is not merely a game but a demonstrable AI simulation, where every system — from the emotion drift model to the NPC seek-steering algorithm — reflects a deliberate application of artificial intelligence principles to a problem that is both technically rigorous and socially relevant.
